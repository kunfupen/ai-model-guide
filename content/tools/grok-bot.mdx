## What it is

Grok Bot is xAI's autonomous agent product, launched August 11, 2026 alongside
[Grok 4.6](/models/xai-grok-4-6). It is not a chat interface and not a coding
assistant — xAI pitches it as **AI teammates you delegate real work to**.

Each bot is an always-on agent with **its own cloud computer**: a browser, a
filesystem and command-line tools, running remotely. You message a bot the way
you'd message a colleague, it signs into the apps you already use, works across
them, and comes back when the job is done or when something needs your
approval. Because the machine is in the cloud, work continues after you close
your laptop.

xAI says it built this as an internal prototype that spread across the company,
with teams standing up bots for sales outbound, marketing campaigns, office
operations and bug fixes.

## What it does well

- **Long-running, unattended work.** The cloud VM is the whole point: tasks that
  take hours don't need your machine awake, and a bot can pick up where it left
  off.
- **Works inside your existing tools.** Rather than requiring API integrations,
  bots sign in and operate apps and inboxes the way a person would.
- **Delegation, not autocomplete.** The unit of work is a job with a finished
  result, not a completion or a diff.
- **Persistent memory.** Bots retain conversation history and adapt to how you
  like things done, so a workflow demonstrated once can be repeated.

## Trade-offs

- **Beta software.** xAI ships it as beta, and stability and feature
  availability were still moving week to week through August 2026. Autonomy is
  gated behind permission controls.
- **The billing model has a sharp edge.** Each eligible plan carries a *weekly
  usage allowance*; beyond it, usage is billed on demand from model and token
  cost, and as of launch there was **no Grok-Bot-specific spend cap**. An agent
  that runs unattended and bills by the token is a combination worth watching.
- **xAI-only.** It runs on Grok, with no option to point it at another
  provider's model. If model choice matters, an agent like
  [Aider](/tools/aider) or [Claude Code](/tools/claude-code) is the
  wrong-shaped but more flexible comparison.
- **No Android at launch.** Desktop (macOS, Windows, Linux) and iOS shipped;
  Android was stated as planned.

## Pricing and access

Grok Bot is not sold standalone — it is bundled into xAI subscriptions, and the
entry price moved sharply after launch:

- **At launch (Aug 11, 2026):** SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus and SuperGrok Heavy;
  Cursor Pro, Pro+ and Ultra; Cursor Teams Standard and Premium.
- **From Aug 26, 2026:** extended to every SuperGrok, Cursor Pro and Cursor
  Teams plan, bringing the entry point down to roughly **$20/month** — reported
  as about a 10× drop from the launch floor.

Reported tier prices run from ~$20/month (Cursor Pro) and ~$30/month
(SuperGrok) up to ~$120/seat/month (Cursor Teams Premium) and ~$300/month
(SuperGrok Heavy). Grok Bot usage draws on its own pool, separate from your
existing plan's usage.

## Who it's for

People who want to hand off whole jobs rather than supervise a coding loop —
operations, research, inbox and campaign work — and who already pay for a
SuperGrok or Cursor subscription. If what you actually want is an agent for a
codebase, xAI's **Grok Build** CLI is the closer fit and is a separate product.
